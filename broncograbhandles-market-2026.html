Why Grab Handles Matter Off-Road

Grab handles are one of those accessories that seem optional until the moment they are not. On trails with significant articulation — where the vehicle tilts 20–30 degrees side to side — passengers instinctively reach for something solid. Without a proper grab handle, that means the A-pillar, the roll bar, the door frame, or the dashboard. None of these are designed to be grip points and none of them provide the bracing leverage that a purpose-built handle does.

Paracord handles specifically were invented by Bartact. The original design emerged from the need for a grip that was strong enough to hold a person's weight under dynamic load, flexible enough not to crack in cold temperatures, and simple enough to wrap and unwrap for color customization. 550 paracord has a minimum break strength of 550 lbs — well above what any passenger will generate bracing on a trail.

Bolt-On vs Slip-On: The Safety Difference

Slip-on grab handles — the ones that hook over the roll bar — are convenient and cheap. They are also the ones that come off when you need them most. At full articulation, when a passenger is putting their full weight and arm strength into the handle, the last thing you want is a design that relies on friction to stay in place.

Bolt-on handles use the factory threaded holes in the roll bar to secure a metal mounting plate through the roll bar. The handle is permanently attached to the bar until you choose to remove it. For 2024 and newer JL Wranglers specifically, bolt-on is not just preferable — it is required. Jeep changed the roll bar design on 2024+ JL models, and slip-on handles that fit older JL models do not fit the new roll bar geometry. Bolt-on designs engineered for the new spec are the only option that works correctly.
